Looking at these three pages, your stack is close but has real gaps for *this specific type* of document. Here's the breakdown:

**What PyMuPDF + EasyOCR + Pillow handles fine:**
- These are scanned/photographed pages, not born-digital PDFs (you can see paper texture, slight skew, and a staple mark on images 1/2). So `fitz` will find zero extractable text on the ledger pages — you'll be in OCR fallback 100% of the time here, not "fallback" in the occasional sense. That's expected and your EasyOCR path handles it.
- Printed tabular text (dates, particulars, amounts) — EasyOCR should read these reasonably well since the font is clean and high-contrast.

**Where this stack will struggle or fail outright:**

1. **Table structure reconstruction** — EasyOCR gives you text + bounding boxes, not rows/columns. Your ledger has 6 columns (Date, Particulars, Vch Type, Vch No, Debit, Credit) and the invoice has a dense multi-column tax table (Taxable Value, CGST/SGST rate+amt, Comp Cess). Without the Y-axis row-pairing + X-axis column-binning logic you and I discussed earlier, you'll get a bag of unordered text fragments, not a structured table. This needs custom spatial-clustering code on top of EasyOCR's output — it's not something the three libraries give you for free.

2. **QR codes** — Image 3 (the tax invoice) has two QR codes (top-right "Electronic Reference Number" area and bottom-left). EasyOCR won't decode these — it's a text OCR engine, not a barcode reader. You'll need `pyzbar` or OpenCV's `QRCodeDetector` added to the stack if that data matters.

3. **Watermark/stamp interference** — Image 1/2 has the circular company stamp overlapping the closing balance row area, and image 3 has a large ghosted Mahindra logo watermark behind the entire invoice table. This bleeds into OCR confidence and can cause misreads or dropped characters on overlapping text. You'll likely need a preprocessing step (contrast/threshold adjustment via OpenCV, not just Pillow) before feeding to EasyOCR.

4. **Handwritten signature** — image 3 has a handwritten signature ("R. Basumatary"). EasyOCR is weak on cursive handwriting — this is exactly why we landed on TrOCR for the handwritten fields in your earlier invoice-extraction pipeline. If you need to verify signature *presence* only, EasyOCR/contour-detection is fine; if you need to transcribe it, you need TrOCR.

5. **Field-label fuzzy matching** — OCR will occasionally misread labels (e.g. "CGST/SAC" vs "CGST/HSN", "Vch No." vs "Vch No"). Without RapidFuzz doing fuzzy matching against a canonical field list, small OCR errors will break any downstream field-mapping logic.

**Bottom line:** your 3-library stack is a good *foundation* but incomplete for production use on these documents. I'd add:
- `OpenCV` — deskew/preprocess before OCR, plus QR decoding
- `RapidFuzz` — fuzzy-match OCR'd labels to canonical field names
- Custom spatial clustering — turn EasyOCR's box outputs into actual table rows/columns (the Y-axis pairing strategy from before)
- `TrOCR` — only if you need to transcribe the handwritten signature, not just detect it
